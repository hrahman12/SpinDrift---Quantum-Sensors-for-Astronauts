Long-duration spaceflight removes Earth's clinical safety net: crews on multi-month journeys have no hospital, no diagnostic lab, and strict consumable budgets. Standard medical hardware breaks down in orbit—adhesive ECG electrodes peel and lose impedance in sweat, liquid-helium MEG scanners cannot fly, and repeated blood draws exhaust sterile supplies. Without resupply or rapid return, the crew medical officer needs continuous, noninvasive cardiac, neurological, and oxidative-stress telemetry that operates reliably without consumables.

SpinDrift pairs three demonstrated, wearable quantum biosensors with an environment-aware medical rules engine. On the chest, a QuSpin QZFM optically pumped magnetometer (OPM) built around a domino-sized ⁸⁷Rb vapor cell (<15 fT/√Hz) performs contactless magnetocardiography—tracking cardiac magnetic field amplitude, QRS morphology, and NPPA wall-stretch correlates, with a 12 mm NV-diamond cardiac magnetometer alternative (6–26 pT/√Hz, demonstrated on real human hearts, arXiv 2601.18843, Jan 2026). On the head, a wearable miniaturized OPM-MEG helmet (12 × 16.5 × 22.5 mm per sensor, <7 fT/√Hz, 130 Hz bandwidth, 0.7 W per head, 49–128 channels, FieldLine HEDscan class) captures alpha/theta fatigue drift and BDNF-linked cognitive stress. On the finger, NV-diamond T₁ relaxometry (532 nm excitation, 637–800 nm readout, 5.3 × 10⁻¹³ M/√Hz ROS sensitivity, demonstrated in living cardiomyocytes) monitors oxidative stress alongside renin and plasma-volume trends.

Unlike classical medical probes, SpinDrift's quantum sensors respond directly to the space magnetic environment around them. SERF (Spin-Exchange Relaxation-Free) OPMs improve as ambient magnetic fields drop toward zero. In low-Earth orbit (~25–65 µT), Earth's geomagnetic field requires shielding or active nulling coils; in cislunar transit, interplanetary flight, and deep space, ambient fields fall into the nanotesla regime (<10 nT down to sub-nanotesla interstellar levels), making Earth-style MEG shielding unnecessary and pushing OPM sensitivity toward peak performance. Europa is the physics exception: Jupiter's ~420 µT ambient field degrades SERF OPM operation, shifting reliance onto resilient NV-diamond relaxometry and surviving compensated MEG channels. SpinDrift models this operational trade-off openly.

Every dashboard metric anchors to open, peer-reviewed science rather than synthetic guesswork. SpinDrift integrates NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR) transcriptomics—OSD-580 for cardiac tissue and OSD-352 for hippocampal neurobiology—alongside OpenNeuro ds004902 (71-subject sleep-deprivation EEG, CC0) and published astronaut physiology from Inspiration4 (OSDR) and ISS cohorts documenting renin elevation, plasma-volume contraction, and retinal thickening in Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS). Every value, dataset, and publication is traced in the application footer. All green, amber, and red boundaries in SpinDrift's shared rules engine are explicitly flagged as prototype hackathon thresholds, not clinically validated diagnostic criteria.

Interactive scenario switching brings this physics-to-physiology bridge to life. Users toggle between TODAY (ISS, Tiangong, Crew Dragon, Orion, Gateway) and TOMORROW (Lunar Base, Mars Transit, Mars Surface, Europa, Deep Space). Moving from the ISS toward Mars Transit or Deep Space raises OPM sensitivity as ambient fields fall while tightening alert windows for radiation and zero-rescue isolation. Selecting Europa drops OPM sensitivity under Jupiter's ~420 µT field and engages NV-diamond fallback. Each destination dynamically updates the calm flight-surgeon crew brief and AI analyst guardrails, stating explicitly where no human crew has flown.
